Icarus

An ongoing exploration of infrastructure, privacy, and building dependable services for everyday use.

This page explains the engineering—not how to access private services. Internal addresses, service endpoints, credentials, and management interfaces are intentionally omitted.

The motivation

Why I built it

I wanted to understand what it takes to operate useful services, not merely deploy them.

Icarus gives me practical experience with infrastructure while keeping control of data and providing useful services for family. It is also a long-term learning environment for networking, identity, monitoring, backups, and recovery.

The goal is simple: private services should be convenient for their intended users without turning the portfolio into a public entrance to my home infrastructure.

Engineering journal

A recovery lesson

Sanitized write-up
Lesson 001 · Recovery design

Recovery cannot depend on the service being recovered

Icarus keeps recovery and identity activation deliberately conservative. An application can be configured in staging while remaining unavailable to users until restore evidence, rollback steps, and an independent access path are ready.

The important lesson was to separate three questions: can data be restored, can an operator regain access safely, and can the service be re-enabled without bypassing those controls? Passing a configuration test does not answer the last two.

Rule of thumb Never make the recovery path depend on the application currently failing.
